    How to convert an AO3 HTML download to EPUB

    1. Download the HTML

      On the work, click Entire Work, then Download → HTML. Logged-in-only works download the same way once you're signed in.

    2. Drop it here

      The title, author, chapters, tags and notes are read straight from the file. Fix the title or pick a cover colour if you like.

    3. Make the EPUB

      Press Make EPUB and save the file. Open it in Apple Books, Kobo, Google Play Books, KOReader — or send it to your Kindle.

    AO3 HTML to EPUB: common questions

    Why is AO3 only giving me HTML right now?

    As of late November 2026, many readers report that AO3 downloads are HTML-only while features are being restored after the recent outage (AO3 came back on November 15 after 17 days offline). Check AO3's official status posts for the latest. HTML is perfectly good input: this page turns it into the EPUB you'd normally get.

    Is my file uploaded anywhere?

    No. The converter is plain JavaScript running on this page. Your HTML file is read in your browser and the EPUB is assembled there, so it never touches a server. No ads, no trackers.

    Do I need Calibre?

    No. Calibre works too, but this page needs no install and works on phones and tablets as well as computers.

    Does this site download fics from AO3 for me?

    No, and on purpose. You download the work yourself through AO3's own button, so the author's hit count and the archive's rules stay intact. Please keep converted files for your own reading and leave kudos and comments on AO3.

    Will it work on my Kindle?

    Yes. Amazon's Send to Kindle takes EPUB files by email, through the Kindle app or on the web, and converts them for your device. Kobo, Apple Books, Google Play Books, Boox and KOReader open the EPUB directly.

    Can I combine a series or several fics?

    Add the HTML files in reading order (use the arrows to reorder), choose Merge into one book and give the book a title. Each work keeps its own chapters under its name in the table of contents.

    Does it work with HTML that isn't from AO3?

    Yes. Any saved HTML page or story works: the converter uses the page title, and if the text has two or more section headings it splits chapters there.

    What happens to images?

    Images that live on other websites can't be fetched from a file, so each one is replaced with a short note giving its description. Text, italics, footnotes and links come through.
